### How do you gather the quotes?
Quotes are fetched from official government websites, for example parliamentary records. For a full list, see the About section. Note that four sources are fetched from mirrors of the official material: the European Council press releases, the Counsil of the European Union press releases, some People's Daily articles and the Legislative Yuan gazette records. Note also that some sources here, like UN Web TV, used automatic speech recognition and are therefore not considered official records. We did not apply automatic speech recognition ourselves. Abridged quotes are checked against the source programmatically. 
### How did you decide which countries are included?
Countries are included based on estimated volume of AI-related discourse and availability of official quote data. [Contact us](lennart@finke.dev) if you would like to see a country or intergovernmental organization added.

### Was AI used in the tagging process?
Yes. Tags are assigned via a concensus of two LLM-based judges, based on definitions from existing taxonomies of AI risk (from the MIT AI Risk Repository) and AI regulation (from the Existential Risk Observatory’s AI Governance and Regulatory Archive), as well as some overarching keywords we defined. For details and definitions of the tags, see the About page.
### Do humans check every quote and tag?
No. We did however spot-check agreement with human ratings. See the About page for details. When a tag seems inaccurate, it could be about the larger utterance the short quote is drawn from; check the source text link to be sure.
