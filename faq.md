### How do you gather the quotes?
Quotes are fetched from official government websites, for example parliamentary records. For a full list, see the About section. Note that four sources are fetched from mirrors of the official material: the European Council press releases, the Counsil of the European Union press releases, some People's Daily articles and the Legislative Yuan gazette records. Abridged quotes are checked against the source programmatically. 
### How did you decide which countries are included?
Countries are included based on volume of AI-related discourse and availability of official quote data. Some sources had to be excluded due to copyright concerns. [Contact us](lennart@finke.dev) if you would like to see a country or intergovernmental organization added.

### Was AI used in the tagging process?
Yes. Tags are assigned via a concensus of two LLM-based judges, based on definitions from existing taxonomies of AI risk (from the MIT AI Risk Repository) and AI regulation (from the Existential Risk Observatory’s AI Governance and Regulatory Archive), as well as some overarching keywords we defined. For details and definitions of the tags, see the About page.
### Do humans check every quote and tag?
No. We did however spot-check agreement with human ratings. See the About page for details. When a tag seems inaccurate, it could be about the larger utterance the short quote is drawn from; check the source text link to be sure. The same applies to quote bylines as speaker profiles.
### Is the quote collection representative or comprehensive?
No, at least not on a country level. For example, if country X has more quotes related to AGI than country B in our data, it could simply be because we missed an important source in country B. However, our data aims to be comprehensive on a source level. For example, our data does definitively show that United Kingdom Parliament debates have more AGI-related discussion than German Parliament debates. Besides the data sourcing, it is also important to consider language differences. For example, it could be that the data misses some AGI-related discussion from Chinese sources because our methodology is not as good at surfacing it in Mandarin.
### Why is there a dropoff in the recent month or months?
Some of the data sources publish with a delay, which can produce an illusion of dropoff of number of quotes in recent months. This is especially true for the United States data (such as the Congress and House hearings), with some delays as long as a year.
